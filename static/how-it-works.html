Method

How it works

A LightGBM model trained on 1.25 million NSW sales, used not to predict a price but to adjust comparable sales — then measured on 119,095 sales it never saw.

The chain, end to end

Six stages. Each one is a script you can re-run — open any step for the detail.

01 Ingest and clean 1.5M raw rows down to 1,249,163 usable residential sales.

1,508,054 rows of NSW Valuer General property sales. Keep residential only, drop part-interest sales and nominal transfers, convert the 6.4% of areas recorded in hectares, de-duplicate. 1,249,163 sales survive.

02 Freeze the split, before any modelling Train, calibrate and test windows fixed and committed up front.

Train on contracts before 2025-01-01, calibrate to 2025-10-01, test on everything after — 119,095 sales, untouched until final scoring.

Cross-validation inside the training window is grouped by ABS statistical area, so a property's neighbours never sit in both the fitting fold and the scoring fold.

03 Engineer features — fitted on training data only Four groups, with the target-derived index fitted on train alone.

Four groups: structural (land area, type), spatial (suburb, statistical area, coordinates, distance to the CBD, zoning), temporal (year, month, days elapsed) and a suburb price index.

The index is derived from the target, so it is computed on the training window alone and then applied everywhere — otherwise test information leaks into training.

04 Train the point model LightGBM on log price, with XGBoost trained alongside as a check.

A market-drift correction is estimated on the calibration window only: the trees cannot extrapolate a price trend past the end of their training data, so they price recent sales at older levels until that level shift is removed.

05 Calibrate the uncertainty Conformalised quantile regression, cross-checked against MAPIE.

Quantile regression at the 10th and 90th percentiles, then conformalised: the raw band is widened by a single constant learned from held-out calibration data, which gives a distribution-free coverage guarantee. The result is checked against MAPIE's independent implementation.

06 Assess the guide against comparable sales 25 nearest sales, adjusted by the model's ratio, then the guide is placed.

A k-nearest-neighbour index returns the 25 most similar recent sales. Each is adjusted to the subject property by the model's ratio between them, and the guide is placed in that adjusted distribution.

adjusted = sale_price × [ model(subject) ÷ model(comparable) ]

Why the comparables are the anchor

This is the design decision that matters most.

The obvious build is: predict a price, compare the guide to it. We built that, measured it, and it was the wrong way round.

On 1,400 sampled test sales, the median of the comparable sales landed at 99.9% of the eventual price, while the model on its own landed at 96.7%. The comparables are the better-centred estimator. So they became the anchor, and the model was demoted to the job it is actually good at — judging how much more or less the subject is worth than each individual comparable:

adjusted comp = comp sale price × [ model(subject) ÷ model(comp) ]

Taking a ratio cancels much of the model's level error: if it reads 8% low on the subject, it usually reads 8% low on a near-identical neighbour too. This is also how a human valuer works — and it is the standard NSW law actually applies, since an agent must justify a guide against comparable sales.

Adjustments are clipped at roughly half-to-double, because past that the two properties are not really comparable and the adjustment is not trustworthy.

The harness was proved before the model existed

A predictions file was fabricated with a deliberate +12% bias and a band engineered to cover 70% against a nominal 80%. The evaluation harness had to recover those numbers; if it had reported 80% coverage there, it would have lied about the real model too. It recovered +11.85% median bias and exactly 70.0% coverage. The probe file is deleted before the real results are scored so it cannot pollute the table.

What failed, and what changed

Both changes came from evidence, not from chasing an accuracy metric.

Failure 1

The binary flag

The first build flagged a guide when it fell below the model's lower bound. Measured on the test set, it caught a real 20% underquote 1 time in 4 while wrongly flagging 1 honest guide in 15. Narrowing the population barely moved it.

The target was wrong, not the accuracy. “Will this sell above the guide?” is not the question — properties routinely sell above guide in a hot auction with nobody underquoting. The flag was deleted and the output rebuilt around comparable sales.

Failure 2

Percentile bands

The replacement banded on the percentile of the adjusted comparables — “strong” below the 5th, “notable” below the 15th. Then we measured the spread of those comparables over 400 properties: the middle half spans about 28% of the median.

So a real 15% underquote landed at the 24th percentile and was reported back as merely “at the low end”. The bands only fired on discounts nobody needed a model to notice. They are now scaled by the segment's measured error instead.

The data

NSW Valuer General Property Sales Information, the public record of every property sale in the state.

1.25M

Sales after cleaning

From 1,508,054 raw rows. Effectively 2019 onward — only about 33,000 rows predate 2019.

6.4%

Areas in hectares

The area column is metres or hectares depending on a second column. Treating them alike is a 10,000× error that silently wrecks every area feature.

687

Rows the “non-market” flag catches

Out of 1.5 million — yet there are 5,198 sales under $10,000. Nominal transfers are cut relative to the suburb × property-type median instead.

What the data does not contain

  • No bedrooms or bathrooms. Not a single column. This is the largest missing feature in the whole project.
  • No latitude or longitude. Only address text. Geocoding 1.2 million addresses through a free service would take over two weeks and breach its usage policy, so every sale is resolved to its suburb centroid — which is why the estimate is suburb-level rather than street-level.
  • No condition, aspect, renovation or view. Two identical-on-paper properties can differ by a great deal in reality.

Reproduce it

Everything in the tables above regenerates from the submitted folder.

python selftest.py   # the demo cases still band correctly (~1 min)
python src/harness.py   # rescore the frozen test set (~1 min)
python src/run_all.py   # rebuild everything from raw data (15–25 min)

run_all.py refuses to overwrite the frozen split, so re-running cannot quietly change the test set.
